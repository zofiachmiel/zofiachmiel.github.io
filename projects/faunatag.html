FaunaTag — Casing Design for a Non-Invasive Whale Tag

Durham, NC · Jan 2026 — Present

FaunaTag is a non-invasive biologging tag developed by FaunaLabs, a nonprofit research organization focused on whale and marine mammal conservation. It attaches to whales and dolphins with a suction-cup mechanism, capturing movement, acoustics, depth and temperature, and bio-optical signals to give researchers a rich view of an animal's experience without disrupting its natural behavior.

As mechanical lead on FaunaTag, I've worked on a series of updates to the tag's casing and mechanisms. A major focus has been the suction-cup attachment — striking the right balance on skin contact: not so tight that it blocks the LEDs from getting a clean read on the whale, but not so loose that the tag risks falling off.

I also helped refine the release mechanism that lets the tag detach on its own: after a programmed window of four to six hours, air is released into the suction cups to break the seal, letting the tag pop free and bob at the surface for recovery.

On the casing itself, I moved to a two-piece design that makes it much easier to manage buoyancy and weight distribution and to get the electronics in and out. I also worked on the tag's magnetic charging connector — a convective, MagSafe-style charger — strengthening it and repositioning it within the case so it holds a solid connection even in rocky, open-ocean boat conditions.

Alongside that, I've built out different casing models to accommodate different sensor configurations. FaunaTag is my favorite project to work on — it sits right at the intersection of engineering, biology, and marine mammal physiology, which is exactly the kind of product design work I want to keep doing.
